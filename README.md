# -BIKE-STORE-CUSTOMER-ANALYSIS
# Bike Store Customer Analysis | Excel

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer demographic and income data from a bike store to understand customer purchasing behaviour, identify regional performance differences, and discover opportunities to improve bike sales.

The analysis focuses on customer income, age, gender, education, region, and bike purchase decisions. The goal is to transform raw customer data into meaningful business insights that can support better marketing and sales strategies.

## Business Objectives

The analysis was conducted to answer the following business questions:

1. What is the total customer income?
2. What is the average customer income?
3. What is the average age of customers?
4. What is the average income by gender?
5. Which region has the highest and lowest number of bike purchases?
6. Which region has the highest total customer income?
7. Which education group has the highest and lowest total income?
8. What strategies can the business implement to improve performance in the lowest-performing region?

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel
- Excel PivotTables
- Excel formulas
- Data cleaning and transformation
- Data analysis and visualization

## Data Preparation

The dataset initially contained 1,026 rows. During data preparation, repeated customer IDs and inconsistent records were addressed, leaving 1,000 unique customer records for analysis.

The preparation process included:

- Removing duplicate customer records.
- Correcting inconsistent values.
- Checking data consistency.
- Preparing the cleaned dataset for analysis.

## Key Findings and Analysis

### 1. Customer Income Analysis

The dataset recorded a total customer income of **56,360,000**, with an average customer income of **56,360**.

This provides an overview of the income profile of the customers included in the analysis.

### 2. Average Income by Gender

| Gender | Customers | Average Income | Total Income |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Male | 511 | 58,062.62 | 29,670,000 |
| Female | 489 | 54,580.78 | 26,690,000 |

**Finding:** Male customers recorded a higher average income and total income than female customers.

**Insight:** The income difference may help the business understand the purchasing power of its customer segments. However, income alone does not establish which gender is more likely to purchase a bike.

### 3. Regional Bike Purchase Analysis

| Region | Customers | Bike Purchases | Purchase Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| North America | 508 | 220 | 43.31% |
| Europe | 300 | 148 | 49.33% |
| Pacific | 192 | 113 | 58.85% |

**Findings:**
- North America recorded the highest number of bike purchases, with 220.
- Pacific recorded the lowest number of purchases, with 113.
- Pacific recorded the highest purchase rate, at 58.85%.
- North America had the largest customer base, with 508 customers.

**Insight:** Although Pacific recorded the lowest purchase count, it had the highest purchase rate. This suggests that its lower purchase count may be associated with its smaller customer base rather than weak customer conversion.

### 4. Total Customer Income by Region

| Region | Total Customer Income |
|---|---:|
| North America | 31,880,000 |
| Europe | 12,270,000 |
| Pacific | 12,210,000 |

**Findings:**
- North America recorded the highest total customer income of 31,880,000.
- Pacific recorded the lowest total customer income of 12,210,000.
- Europe's total customer income was 12,270,000.

**Insight:** North America contributes the largest share of total customer income in the dataset. Pacific recorded the lowest total, slightly below Europe.

### 5. Income Analysis by Education

| Education | Total Customer Income | Average Income |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bachelors | 19,280,000 | 63,006.54 |
| Partial College | 14,500,000 | 54,716.98 |
| Graduate Degree | 11,500,000 | 66,091.95 |
| High School | 8,460,000 | 47,262.57 |
| Partial High School | 2,620,000 | 34,473.68 |

**Findings:**
- Bachelors recorded the highest total customer income of 19,280,000.
- Partial High School recorded the lowest total customer income of 2,620,000.
- Graduate Degree customers recorded the highest average income of 66,091.95.
- Partial High School customers recorded the lowest average income of 34,473.68.

**Insight:** Customers with a Bachelor's degree contributed the highest combined income, while customers with a Graduate Degree had the highest average income per customer. This shows why total income and average income should be interpreted separately.

## Business Insights

The analysis revealed the following important insights:

1. **North America is the strongest region by scale:** It has the largest customer base, the highest number of bike purchases, and the highest total customer income.

2. **Pacific presents an opportunity for customer-base expansion:** Although it has the lowest purchase count and total income, it has the highest purchase rate of 58.85%. The business should focus on reaching more potential customers in this region.

3. **Male customers have higher average income:** Male customers recorded an average income of 58,062.62 compared with 54,580.78 for female customers. This difference can inform further customer segmentation.

4. **Education groups show different income patterns:** Bachelor's degree customers contribute the highest total income, while Graduate Degree customers have the highest average income.

5. **There is room to improve overall purchasing activity:** Out of 1,000 customers, 481 purchased bikes, representing an overall purchase rate of 48.1%.

## Recommendations

### 1. Expand Customer Reach in the Pacific Region

The business should increase awareness of its products in the Pacific region through targeted advertising, local partnerships, cycling events, and test-ride campaigns.

Since Pacific already has the highest purchase rate, expanding the number of potential customers reached could help increase total purchases and customer income.

### 2. Investigate Customers Who Have Not Purchased

The business should identify customers who have not purchased bikes and investigate the factors influencing their decisions.

Customer feedback and segmentation by age, income, education, and commute distance can help identify potential barriers to purchasing.

### 3. Introduce Targeted Promotions

The business should consider suitable promotional offers, product bundles, discounts, and flexible payment options where financially feasible.

These strategies may encourage interested customers to complete purchases. However, customer feedback should first establish whether price is a significant barrier.

### 4. Develop Region-Specific Marketing Strategies

Rather than applying the same marketing strategy across every region, the business should consider differences in customer numbers, purchasing behaviour, and income profiles.

North America can be used as a benchmark for customer reach, while Europe and Pacific can be assessed for specific growth opportunities.

### 5. Monitor Sales and Customer Performance

The business should regularly monitor:
- Total bike purchases.
- Purchase rate.
- Total customer income.
- Average customer income.
- Regional purchasing performance.
- Marketing campaign performance.

This will help management evaluate whether the implemented strategies are producing measurable improvements.

## Conclusion

The analysis shows that North America leads in customer numbers, bike purchases, and total customer income. Pacific recorded the lowest total customer income and lowest number of purchases, but it also achieved the highest purchase rate.

The findings suggest that the business should focus on expanding its customer reach in Pacific, understanding non-buyers, and developing targeted marketing strategies. By using customer and regional insights to guide decisions, the business can identify opportunities to improve purchasing activity and strengthen overall performance.
